# Kiwi UI Sound Effects (SFX) Guide

This folder holds audio files for Kiwi's UI sound effects.
All audio files must be MP3 format with standard 44.1kHz or 48kHz stereo/mono encoding.

## Sound Categories & File Names

Kiwi looks for the following specific sound files in this directory:

1. Category: "Scroll"
   Filename: scroll.mp3
   Event: Fired when scrolling lists, multi-page carousels, or document views.
   Recommended sound: A very soft, subtle tactile tick, flutter, or paper-friction click (~30-60ms).

2. Category: "Enter"
   Filename: enter.mp3
   Event: Fired when entering a major tool, confirming an action, clicking primary action buttons
          (e.g., "Convert File", "Scan", "Apply Crop", "Merge PDF").
   Recommended sound: A crisp, pleasant Apple-like positive affirmation click or soft chime (~80-150ms).

3. Category: "Back"
   Filename: back.mp3
   Event: Fired when navigating back, canceling an action, clearing selections, closing modals,
          or closing the camera scanner view.
   Recommended sound: A gentle descending tone, subtle dismiss tap, or soft pop (~70-120ms).

4. Category: "Failed/Cannot be Accessed"
   Filename: failed.mp3
   Event: Fired when a file conversion fails, a download cannot be retrieved, camera access is denied,
          or an invalid file/format combination is encountered.
   Recommended sound: A gentle, non-jarring low alert or double-tap error tone (~150-250ms).

5. Category: "Notification"
   Filename: notification.mp3
   Event: Fired when an informational or success toast notification pops up, or when an offline job
          finishes processing in the background.
   Recommended sound: A crisp, friendly glass ding, soft marimba chord, or clean bubble chime (~120-250ms).

## Graceful Fallback Behavior

- If any or all of these MP3 files are missing from this folder, Kiwi continues to work 100% normally and silently.
- No network errors, 404 console warnings, or user-facing alerts will appear if audio files are absent.
- Audio volume is kept gentle (35-45% master volume) to maintain a quiet, calm offline workspace aesthetic.
